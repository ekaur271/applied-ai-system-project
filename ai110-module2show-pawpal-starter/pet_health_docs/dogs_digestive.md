# Dog Digestive Health

## Eating Grass
Dogs eat grass for several reasons. Most commonly it signals mild stomach upset — the grass induces vomiting to clear the stomach. Occasional grass eating with no other symptoms is usually harmless. Frequent grass eating combined with vomiting, lethargy, or loss of appetite may indicate gastritis or gastrointestinal irritation and warrants a vet visit.

## Vomiting
Occasional vomiting (once or twice) after eating too fast or eating something unusual is common and usually not serious. Vomiting more than twice in 24 hours, vomiting blood, or vomiting combined with lethargy or diarrhea is serious and requires immediate veterinary attention. Chronic vomiting can indicate food allergies, inflammatory bowel disease, or intestinal obstruction.

## Diarrhea
Mild diarrhea for less than 24 hours is often caused by dietary indiscretion (eating something new or unusual). Withhold food for 12 hours and offer fresh water. If diarrhea persists beyond 24 hours, contains blood, or is accompanied by vomiting or lethargy, see a vet. Chronic soft stools may indicate food intolerance or intestinal parasites.

## Loss of Appetite
A dog skipping one meal is rarely alarming. If a dog refuses food for more than 24 hours it can indicate dental pain, nausea, infection, or a more serious systemic issue. Puppies and small dogs are at higher risk of hypoglycemia from missed meals and should be seen sooner.

## Bloating and Distended Abdomen
A bloated, hard, or distended abdomen especially in large or deep-chested breeds (Great Danes, German Shepherds) can indicate gastric dilatation-volvulus (GDV), which is life-threatening. Signs include unproductive retching, restlessness, and a visibly swollen belly. This is an emergency — seek veterinary care immediately.

## Constipation
Straining to defecate or no bowel movements for more than two days may indicate constipation. Causes include dehydration, low fiber diet, or ingestion of foreign material. Mild cases may resolve with increased water intake. Persistent straining or crying when defecating requires veterinary evaluation.

## Excessive Drooling or Lip Licking
Sudden excessive drooling or repeated lip licking often signals nausea in dogs. It can also indicate oral pain, a foreign object stuck in the mouth, or exposure to a toxic substance. Check the mouth if safe to do so and monitor for vomiting.
