# Cat Behavioral Health

## Hiding
Cats hide when they feel unwell, stressed, or threatened. A cat that suddenly hides for most of the day and avoids interaction is showing a classic sign of illness or pain. Combined with other symptoms (not eating, lethargy) this warrants a vet visit within 24 hours.

## Excessive Vocalization
Sudden increase in meowing, yowling especially at night can indicate pain, cognitive dysfunction in older cats, hyperthyroidism, or sensory decline (hearing/vision loss). Unspayed females yowling may be in heat.

## Lethargy
A cat sleeping more than its usual 12-16 hours, or that is unresponsive to play and interaction, may be experiencing illness. Baseline lethargy combined with reduced appetite is one of the most common presentations of systemic illness in cats.

## Grooming Changes
Over-grooming (excessive licking leading to bald patches) often indicates stress, allergies, or skin irritation. Under-grooming (a dull, matted, or unkempt coat) is a sign the cat feels unwell or is in pain — cats are fastidious groomers and a poorly groomed coat is a reliable illness indicator.

## Litter Box Avoidance
A cat that stops using the litter box may have a urinary tract infection, constipation, or joint pain that makes climbing into the box difficult. Urinating outside the box on cool surfaces (tiles, bathtubs) strongly suggests urinary discomfort. Rule out medical causes before assuming behavioral issues.

## Aggression or Sudden Personality Change
A cat that becomes suddenly aggressive toward people or other pets it previously tolerated is often in pain. Dental disease, arthritis, and internal pain are common hidden causes of aggression in cats. A veterinary exam is warranted before behavioral intervention.

## Excessive Scratching or Head Shaking
Frequent head shaking, scratching at ears, or a dark discharge in the ears indicates ear mites or an ear infection. Left untreated, ear infections can spread to the inner ear and affect balance.
