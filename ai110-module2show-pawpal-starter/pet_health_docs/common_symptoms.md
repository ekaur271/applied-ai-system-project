# Common Symptoms Across Pets

## Severity Classification Guide

### Low Severity
Symptoms that are isolated, mild, and short-lived. Monitor at home for 24 hours. No immediate vet visit required unless symptoms worsen or persist.
- Occasional vomiting (once, no blood)
- Mild diarrhea (less than 24 hours, no blood)
- Skipping one meal
- Mild lethargy after exercise
- Occasional grass eating in dogs

### Medium Severity
Symptoms that persist, recur, or combine with other signs. Schedule a vet appointment within 24-48 hours.
- Vomiting more than twice in 24 hours
- Diarrhea lasting more than 24 hours
- Refusing food for more than 24 hours
- Excessive thirst or urination
- Persistent scratching or skin changes
- Limping without obvious wound
- Behavior change lasting more than a day

### High Severity — Seek Veterinary Care Immediately
Symptoms that indicate a potentially life-threatening condition.
- Vomiting blood or diarrhea with blood
- Bloated or distended abdomen (especially in dogs)
- Straining to urinate producing nothing (especially in male cats)
- Collapse, inability to stand, or extreme weakness
- Seizures
- Difficulty breathing
- Pale or white gums
- Suspected poisoning or toxin ingestion

## When to Go to an Emergency Vet
Go immediately if your pet shows: inability to breathe normally, collapse or unresponsiveness, suspected poisoning, uncontrolled bleeding, suspected broken bones, seizures lasting more than 2 minutes, or straining to urinate with no output (cats especially).

## General Monitoring Tips
- Keep a log of when symptoms started, frequency, and any diet or environment changes
- Note whether the pet is still drinking water
- Check gum color — healthy gums are pink and moist; pale, white, or blue gums are an emergency
- Bring a stool or vomit sample to the vet if possible
- Never give human medication (ibuprofen, acetaminophen, aspirin) to pets — these are toxic
