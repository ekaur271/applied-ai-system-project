# Dog Behavioral Health

## Lethargy and Low Energy
A dog that is suddenly less playful, sleeping more than usual, or reluctant to go on walks may be experiencing pain, illness, or depression. Mild lethargy after intense exercise is normal. Lethargy lasting more than 24 hours or combined with other symptoms (vomiting, coughing, not eating) should be evaluated by a vet.

## Excessive Thirst and Urination
Drinking noticeably more water than usual and urinating frequently can indicate diabetes, kidney disease, Cushing's disease, or a urinary tract infection. This is a common early sign of several serious but manageable conditions and should be investigated promptly.

## Scratching, Licking Paws, or Skin Irritation
Constant scratching, paw licking, or rubbing the face often indicates allergies — either environmental (pollen, dust mites) or food-related. Skin redness, hot spots, or hair loss alongside scratching may indicate a secondary skin infection requiring treatment.

## Limping or Reluctance to Bear Weight
Sudden limping can result from a thorn, cut, or sprain. Inspect the paw for visible injury. If no obvious cause is found or the dog refuses to bear weight at all, a vet should rule out fractures, ligament tears, or joint disease. Gradual onset limping in older dogs often points to arthritis.

## Aggression or Sudden Behavior Change
A normally friendly dog that becomes snappy, growling, or withdrawn is often in pain. Sudden behavior change without an obvious environmental trigger should be treated as a medical concern until ruled out. Neurological conditions and thyroid issues can also cause personality changes.

## Hiding or Withdrawal
Dogs that suddenly hide, avoid contact, or seek isolated spots are often experiencing pain, fear, or illness. This is particularly significant if combined with other symptoms. It is an instinctive behavior when a dog feels vulnerable.

## Excessive Barking or Whining
New onset of persistent vocalization, especially at night, can indicate pain, cognitive decline in older dogs (canine cognitive dysfunction), anxiety, or a medical condition affecting hearing or vision.
